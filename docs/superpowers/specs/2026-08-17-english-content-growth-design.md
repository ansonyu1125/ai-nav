# English Content Growth Design

## Objective

Grow qualified organic traffic from English-speaking professionals and creators by turning AINav from a broad AI tool directory into a trusted decision platform. Phase one publishes approximately 100 high-quality English pages and deepens coverage of 50-80 relevant products without producing thin, automatically generated content.

Chinese content remains available, but new work is written in native English first. Only strategically important or proven English articles are translated into Chinese.

## Audience And Topics

The primary reader knows the task they need to complete but is uncertain which AI product to choose. Initial topics are writing, presentations, meetings, research, image editing, video creation, social media, resumes, translation, and productivity automation.

Content must answer a concrete decision or workflow question. Evergreen, high-intent pages take priority over general AI explainers and news.

## Growth Model

Use task-centered topic clusters as the primary model, supported by hands-on editorial testing. Programmatic pages are allowed only when their structured evidence is complete and verified.

Each task cluster contains:

1. Best pages: which products deserve consideration.
2. Comparisons: how credible options differ.
3. Alternatives: how to replace a known product.
4. Pricing pages: plans, limits, and value.
5. Reviews: actual performance and fit.
6. Tutorials: how to complete the task after choosing.

The user journey is:

`Search query -> task or Best page -> comparison or review -> product detail -> official website`

Each page targets one primary intent, connects to the cluster hub and next step, and links to three to eight verified products where relevant.

## Product Coverage Tiers

### Tier 1: Hands-On Tested

Approximately 30 core products receive hands-on testing with a category-specific standard task set. Testing records output quality, speed, workflow friction, limitations, free allowance, export behavior, and paid restrictions. These products may appear prominently in Best, Review, and Comparison pages.

### Tier 2: Official Sources Verified

Approximately 50 products are verified against official websites, pricing pages, help centers, terms, platform destinations, and changelogs. They may appear in Best and Alternatives pages but must not be described as hands-on tested.

### Tier 3: Watchlist

Products with incomplete information remain discoverable but do not receive prominent rankings or dedicated decision pages. Downgrade or remove products when their official site is unavailable, development appears abandoned, or important facts cannot be verified.

## Required Product Evidence

Every Tier 1 or Tier 2 record must include:

- Official name, logo, company, and product URL
- Plain-language positioning and intended audience
- Core capabilities and meaningful limitations
- Free plan, paid starting price, and billing period
- Official pricing source and verification date
- Supported platforms and material regional restrictions
- Commercial-use and licensing information when relevant
- Three to five meaningful alternatives
- Most recent significant update
- Evidence label: hands-on tested or official sources verified

Missing evidence remains visibly unknown. It must not be inferred from competitors, third-party summaries, or stale cached content.

## Editorial Quality Standard

Every article must:

- Lead with a useful conclusion instead of a generic explanation of AI.
- State its evaluation method and last checked date.
- Compare products across consistent, decision-relevant dimensions.
- Explain who each recommendation is and is not for.
- Cite official sources for pricing, limits, licensing, and availability.
- Distinguish hands-on findings from official-source research.
- Avoid invented experience, scores, usage numbers, market share, or claims.
- Include a useful decision table, selection path, or repeatable workflow.
- Use native English composition rather than literal translation.
- Keep rankings independent from affiliate compensation.
- Link to the cluster hub, product pages, related comparisons, and a next step.

Each category uses a repeatable test protocol. For example, AI writing tools use the same rewriting, long-form drafting, factual-grounding, and tone-control tasks so conclusions remain consistent across pages.

## Publishing Plan

### Batch 1: Establish Authority

Publish 30 pages and complete Tier 1 coverage for approximately 30 products:

| Cluster | Pages | Coverage |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| AI writing | 7 | Best, free tools, comparisons, rewriting, marketing copy |
| Presentations | 6 | Gamma, Canva, Beautiful.ai, teaching, sales |
| Meetings | 6 | Otter, Fireflies, Fathom, Zoom, Teams |
| Research | 6 | Perplexity, NotebookLM, Elicit, citations |
| Image editing | 5 | Canva, Adobe Firefly, Photoroom, Remove.bg |

### Batch 2: Expand Coverage

Publish 35 pages:

- AI video: 8
- Social media content: 7
- Translation and localization: 6
- Resumes and job search: 7
- Productivity automation: 7

Add approximately 25 Tier 2 products. Expand comparisons and alternatives only where Batch 1 query or engagement data supports them.

### Batch 3: Search-Led Expansion

Publish 35-55 pages based on observed demand. Candidate pages require at least one signal:

- Search Console queries ranking in positions 8-30
- Strong click-through from a Best page to a product or comparison
- Repeated site searches with weak or missing results
- Strong outbound intent from pricing or comparison pages
- A significant new product or major update
- A clear audience subsegment revealed by earlier content

Target content mix:

- Best and task pages: 20%
- Comparisons: 20%
- Alternatives: 15%
- Pricing: 10%
- Reviews: 15%
- Tutorials and workflows: 20%

Publish four to six English articles per week. Avoid large same-day batches.

## Editorial Workflow

1. Validate the search intent, reader, target query, and content gap.
2. Gather primary evidence from official sites, pricing, help, terms, and changelogs.
3. Run the category test protocol for Tier 1 products and retain structured notes.
4. Write the conclusion, decision framework, and audience fit in native English.
5. Verify pricing, limits, licensing, platform, and regional claims.
6. Add intentional links within the topic cluster and product graph.
7. Check structured data, mobile layout, outbound links, indexability, and duplication.
8. Schedule periodic verification.

Public evidence states use consistent labels:

- `Hands-on tested`
- `Official sources verified`
- `Last checked: YYYY-MM-DD`
- `Update required`

## Maintenance

- Pricing pages: verify monthly.
- Tier 1 reviews and comparisons: retest quarterly.
- Other evergreen pages: verify at least every six months.
- Broken official URLs and major product changes: review when detected.

When evidence becomes stale, mark it for update rather than retaining an authoritative label.

## Success Metrics

- Percentage of published pages indexed by Google
- Non-branded organic clicks
- Queries and pages ranking in the top 10
- Best-page visits continuing to a product detail
- Product-detail visits continuing to an official website
- Count of stale prices and broken official links
- Percentage of pages in each cluster receiving organic traffic

## Phase-One Acceptance Criteria

- All 30 Batch 1 English pages pass editorial and technical checks.
- Approximately 30 Tier 1 products have completed standard tests or an accurate official-source-only label.
- Material facts have a primary source and verification date.
- No page is published solely by filling a template with incomplete data.
- Internal links form a coherent path from task discovery to product decision.
- Search Console and on-site behavior data approve later topics.

## Non-Goals

- Importing hundreds of tools before core records are trustworthy
- Generating Review, Pricing, or Comparison pages for every database entry
- Using generic AI news as the primary SEO strategy
- Translating every English article at publication time
- Allowing affiliate relationships to determine rankings
