A prompt is the bridge between you and AI. With the same model, the quality of your prompt can make a huge difference in the response. This tutorial teaches you how to write high-quality prompts.

## 1. Four Elements of a Good Prompt

A complete prompt usually consists of four parts:

- **Role**: What identity should the AI assume, e.g., "You are a senior interviewer"
- **Task**: The specific thing to accomplish, e.g., "Help me optimize this self-introduction"
- **Requirements**: Constraints on format, length, tone, etc., e.g., "Keep it under 100 words, conversational"
- **Context**: Relevant background information

## 2. Common Techniques

### 1. Role Setting

> You are a senior product manager with 10 years of experience. Please help me evaluate whether this feature is worth pursuing.

Role setting makes the AI's response more targeted.

### 2. Structured Instructions

Break down your needs into clear items, making it less likely for the AI to miss anything:

- Summarize the three core points of this article
- Summarize each point in one sentence
- Finally, give your evaluation

### 3. Chain of Thought

For complex problems, let the AI think step by step:

> Please reason step by step: first analyze the conditions, then give the conclusion, and finally explain the reasons.

### 4. Few-Shot Examples

Give one or two examples for the AI to mimic the format and style, which is more effective than just describing.

## 3. Common Pitfalls

- Prompts that are too vague: Change "write an article" to "write an 800-word WeChat article on time management, targeting new professionals."
- Asking too many questions at once: Break multiple questions into separate ones for better responses.
- Not iterating: Good answers often come from multiple rounds of follow-up questions. Don't expect to get it right in one go.

> Remember: A prompt is not a magic spell, but a communication skill to clearly express your needs.
